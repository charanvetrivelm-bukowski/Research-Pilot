# Learning notes: research workflow and evidence quality

## Decompose the question

A broad research question is easier to investigate when split into sub-questions about definitions, mechanisms, current approaches, trade-offs, and evidence gaps.

## Record evidence

For each finding, keep the source name and the relevant passage. A source filename is a useful traceability hint, but it is not equivalent to a complete bibliographic citation. For formal work, record author, title, publication, date, DOI or URL, and page or section when available.

## Evaluate evidence

Check whether a passage directly supports the claim, whether it is current enough for the topic, whether the source is authoritative, and whether the claim needs corroboration. Separate source-supported facts from interpretation and assumptions.

## Report uncertainty

If the supplied documents do not support a conclusion, say so. Do not treat a missing search result as proof that something does not exist. List what additional evidence would be needed.

## Limitations of keyword retrieval

Keyword retrieval can miss relevant passages that use synonyms or different terminology. It can also return passages that share words but do not answer the question. For high-quality research, inspect the original sources and consider semantic retrieval and systematic source selection.
